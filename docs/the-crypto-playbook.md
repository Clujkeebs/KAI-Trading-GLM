# Chapter 1: How I Search

Every trade starts with research. Not gut feelings, not Twitter hype, not what your friend said. Research. Here is the exact process I use to find coins before they move.

## Step 1: Identify the Narrative

Crypto moves in narratives, not individual coins. Before searching for specific coins, I identify which sectors have momentum. The major narratives in 2026 are: AI (artificial intelligence agents, DePIN, GPU rendering), RWA (real-world asset tokenization, treasury bonds on-chain), DeFi (decentralized exchanges, lending, yield trading), and L1 (layer-1 blockchains competing with Ethereum). Within each narrative, there are leaders and laggards. The goal is to find laggards in strong narratives before they catch up to the leaders.

For example, when AERO (DeFi DEX on Base) is up 17% in a day, that signals the DeFi narrative has momentum. The next question is: which other DeFi coins have not moved yet? That is where the opportunity lives. You are not chasing what already pumped. You are finding what has not pumped yet in a sector that is clearly moving.

## Step 3: Cross-Reference at Least 3 Sources

Never trust a single source. When I evaluated SOL for your portfolio, I read results from Kraken, CoinMarketCap, CoinStats, TradingView, Changelly, and BitcoinFoundation. Each source has different biases. Kraken wants you to buy (they show staking APY). Changelly publishes aggressive predictions. CoinMarketCap gives balanced analysis. TradingView shows what traders are actually doing. By reading all of them, you get a 360-degree view instead of a single angle.

## Step 4: Verify It Is Actually on Kraken

This sounds obvious but it is the most common mistake. I recommended GRASS as a buy, and it was not on Kraken. I recommended ARB before fully checking its price, and it turned out to be at $0.09 (down 96% from ATH) instead of a recovery play. Every recommendation must pass this gate: Is it listed on Kraken? Can you actually buy it? What is the real current price right now? Never assume. Always verify.

# Chapter 2: How I Decide What Is Good or Bad

After research, most coins fall into one of three categories. Understanding these categories is the single most important skill in crypto trading. More important than any chart pattern or technical indicator.

## The Three Categories

### Category 1: Has Not Pumped Yet (BUY)

These are coins in strong sectors that have not had their explosive move. They are consolidating, building fundamentals, and waiting for a catalyst. AVAX at $7.51 is the perfect example. It is in the L1 sector (strong narrative), down 50% YTD (has not pumped), but revenue is up 460% and smart money is accumulating (fundamentals improving). The setup is: strong sector + depressed price + improving fundamentals = buy candidate.

### Category 2: Already Pumped and Crashed (AVOID)

These are coins that already had their moment and are now bleeding. ARB at $0.09 (down 96% from $2.39 ATH). ENA at $0.09 (down 94% from $1.52 ATH). JUP at $0.18 (down 91% from $2.00 ATH). The mistake people make is thinking these are cheap because they are low in dollar terms. They are not cheap. They are destroyed. The market already moved on. Avoid them completely unless there is a very specific, very recent catalyst that changes the thesis.

### Category 3: Already Pumping Hard (HOLD or TRIM)

These are coins that are up 10-20% in a day. AERO at +17% or RENDER at +16%. These are your winners. The instinct is to take profits immediately, but that is often wrong. If a coin is in a strong narrative and just had a big green day, it usually continues for days or weeks. The right move is usually to hold, unless it has gone parabolic (up 50%+ in a week) or you have a specific reason to trim (like MORPHO with a predicted pullback to $1.53).

## The Evaluation Checklist

For every coin, I run through this mental checklist before recommending a buy, hold, or sell decision. Every single item matters. Missing even one can lead to a bad trade.

| Check | Good Signal | Bad Signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sector Narrative | In AI, RWA, or DeFi (current momentum) | No clear narrative or dead narrative |
| Price vs ATH | Below 50% of ATH (room to recover) | Down 90%+ with no catalyst (probably dead) |
| Analyst Consensus | Multiple sources agree on upside | Sources disagree wildly or predict decline |
| Fundamentals | Revenue, users, or TVL growing | Declining metrics, no development activity |
| 24h Performance | Moving with or ahead of market | Flat or red while market rallies |
| Institutional Adoption | Real companies building on it | Only retail speculation |
| Kraken Listing | Confirmed available on Kraken | Not on Kraken or delisted |
| Risk/Reward | 2x+ upside, 30% downside | Limited upside, huge downside |

# Chapter 3: How I Decide to Sell

Selling is harder than buying. Most people hold losers too long and sell winners too early. Here are the exact rules I use to make sell decisions.

## Rule 1: Sell Dead Money

If a coin goes flat or negative while the entire market is rallying 5-17%, that coin is dead money. INJ at 0% while everything else was green was the clearest sell signal possible. That money is trapped. Free it and deploy it into something that actually moves. The opportunity cost of holding dead money is massive in a strong market.

## Rule 2: Sell Destroyed Coins

If a coin is down 90%+ from its ATH with no specific catalyst for recovery, it is probably dead. ARB at $0.09, ENA at $0.09, JUP at $0.18. These had their moment. The market moved on. Holding them is hoping for a miracle. Sell and move on. There is always another opportunity.

## Rule 3: Trim When Overextended

If a coin has had a massive run and short-term predictions call for a pullback, sell half. Not all. Half. This locks in profits while keeping exposure if the uptrend continues. MORPHO at $2.33 with a predicted pullback to $1.53 was a perfect trim candidate. You sell half, keep half, and you win either way.

## Rule 4: Stop Losses on Lottery Tickets

Small speculative positions (OCEAN, WLD) need hard stop losses. OCEAN below $0.10 = sell. WLD below $0.25 = sell. These are high-risk bets, not core holdings. If the thesis breaks, you get out immediately. No hoping, no averaging down. The loss on a $20 position does not hurt you. Holding a losing lottery ticket to zero does.

## Rule 5: Never Sell Winners on a Green Day

If AERO is up 17% in a day because the DeFi narrative is strong, that is not a sell signal. That is confirmation that you are right. The time to sell winners is when the narrative fades, not when they are proving you correct. Sell losers, not winners.

## The Sell Decision Matrix

| Situation | Action | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat/red in a green market | SELL all | Dead money, opportunity cost |
| Down 90%+ with no catalyst | SELL all | Probably dead, market moved on |
| Overextended, pullback predicted | SELL half | Lock profits, keep exposure |
| Lottery ticket hits stop loss | SELL all | Thesis broken, cut the loss |
| Winner in strong narrative | HOLD all | Confirmation, let it run |
| Winner goes parabolic | TRIM half | Take some off the table |
| Lagging sector rotation | WATCH 1 week | Give it time, then decide |

# Chapter 4: How I Predict What Will Explode

No one can predict the future with certainty. But you can stack probabilities in your favor by understanding how crypto markets work. Here is the framework I use.

## The Sector Rotation Model

Crypto does not move all at once. Money rotates between sectors in a predictable cycle. In July 2026, RWA tokens posted a +10.7% median return, the strongest of any narrative. That means RWA was leading. In August 2026, AI and DeFi started catching up. The pattern is: one sector leads, then money flows to the next underserved sector, then the next. Your job is to identify which sector is leading, and then find the laggards in the next sector that will receive the rotation.

When I saw AERO (DeFi) up 17% and MORPHO (DeFi lending) up 9.6%, that told me DeFi was receiving rotation. So I looked for other DeFi coins that had not moved yet. UNI was barely up, had just launched on Circle Arc, and was the largest DEX by volume. That was the laggard in the receiving sector. That is how you find what will explode next.

## The Coiled Spring Pattern

This is one of the most powerful setups in crypto. A coiled spring is a coin where fundamentals are improving dramatically but the price has not caught up yet. AVAX was the textbook example: revenue up 460% year-over-year, record transaction volumes, Mastercard and Western Union building on it, but the price was still at $6.50. That is a coiled spring. The fundamentals are pulling the price up like a compressed spring. When it releases, the move is fast and large.

## The Smart Money Signal

When multiple independent sources report that institutional or smart money is accumulating a coin, pay attention. AltcoinBuzz reported smart money was buying AVAX near weekly lows. BraveNewCoin noted bullish divergence with $833 million in daily volume. These are not random Twitter posts. These are data-driven observations from credible analysis sources. When the smart money signal aligns with the coiled spring pattern, the probability of a breakout is very high.

## The Staking Yield Floor

Coins with staking yields have a mathematical advantage. SOL at $87 with 5.63% APY means the price can drop 5% and you still break even for the year. That yield acts as a floor during market dips and compounds over time. When you are evaluating two coins with similar upside potential, always prefer the one with staking yield. It reduces your downside risk for free. For a long-term hold, staking yield turns time into your ally instead of your enemy.

## The Price Target Framework

When I evaluate price predictions, I look at the range, not the average. If five analysts say a coin will hit $150 and two say it will hit $50, the range is $50-$150. I then ask: where is the current price within that range? If the coin is at $87 and the range is $50-$150, it is in the lower half. That means more analysts see upside than downside. The wider the range, the more uncertainty. A tight range like $80-$100 means high confidence. A wide range like $20-$300 means nobody knows.

## The Portfolio Management Rules

Rule 1: Diversify across sectors, not coins. Having 15 DeFi coins is not diversification. Having 3 AI, 2 RWA, 2 DeFi, 1 L1, and 1 payment coin is diversification. When AI dumps, your RWA and DeFi positions protect you. This is why your final portfolio has 9 coins across 6 sectors.

Rule 2: One anchor position. For a $1,000 portfolio, one large stable position (SOL at 80%) provides a foundation that does not crash on a bad day. The remaining 20% in smaller positions provides the growth upside. Without an anchor, you are just gambling on small coins.

Rule 3: Never go 100% in. Always keep some cash. Even $5-10 in cash means you are not forced to sell a position at a bad time if you need to adjust. Cash is optionality.

Rule 4: Check weekly, not hourly. Crypto moves in weekly and monthly cycles, not hourly ones. Checking your portfolio every hour leads to emotional decisions. Set a weekly alarm. Look at your positions. Make decisions calmly. Then close the app.

Rule 5: Size positions by conviction, not by dollar amount. Your highest-conviction play (SOL) gets the most money. Medium conviction (AVAX) gets less. Lottery tickets (OCEAN, WLD) get the least. This is how professionals manage risk.

# Final Rules

1. Do your own research. This document teaches you how to think, not what to buy. The market changes every day. The framework stays the same. Apply it yourself.

2. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose. Your $2,500 savings account is your safety net. Never touch it for crypto. Only invest what you can lose without it affecting your life.

3. Time is your biggest advantage. At 12 years old, you have something most traders do not have: time. A 3-year hold for you is nothing. A 3-year hold for a 40-year-old is stressful. Use that advantage. Stake, wait, compound.

4. Stop loss > hope loss. If a position drops below your stop loss, sell immediately. Do not hope it comes back. Hope is not a strategy. Small losses are tuition. Large losses are account killers.

5. The best trade is often no trade. After building your portfolio, the right move was nothing for two weeks. Churning positions every day is how beginners lose money. Build, stake, wait. Let the market come to you.
